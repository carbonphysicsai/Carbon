01

Cooler AI Compute

More compute. Less pumping.

Industrial use

Screen cold-plate channel designs for AI servers with hotspot-temperature and pumping constraints.

Impact to prove

Lower pumping demand at the same thermal requirement.

Illustrative annual customer value

Spend less on electricity for cooling pumps.

One deployment with 1 MW of average cooling-pump electrical demand

If the customer achieves10%less pump electricity at the same cooling duty
→
The example is worthUS$87,600/yrless electricity spend per year

Who benefits: Data-centre operator.

To turn this into money: The operator must reduce metered electrical demand across the installed cooling loop while meeting the same chip-temperature and heat-removal requirements.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:
Assume pump demand falls from 1,000 to 900 kW for 8,760 hours/year, at US$0.10/kWh. This is pump power, not server power or total cooling power.
Calculation:
100 kW × 8,760 hours/year × US$0.10/kWh = US$87,600/year.
Financial meaning:
Avoided operating spend. A reduction in actual recurring spending, before adoption costs and Carbon fees.
Proof needed:
Measure flow, pressure drop, pump efficiency and electrical power at realistic loads. Deduct any higher cold-plate, installation, maintenance or reliability cost.
Technical context:
ARPA-E: COOLERCHIPS ↗. This source supports the mechanism, not these assumed gains or dollar inputs.

Hypothetical example, not a Carbon result or forecast. Deduct Carbon fees and all adoption costs. Customer benefit is not Carbon revenue.

02

AI Compute Efficiency

Find the thermal room to move.

Industrial use

Compare chip power schedules to predict temperature limits, burst duration and recovery.

Impact to prove

More useful compute within independently checked temperature limits.

Illustrative annual customer value

Complete the same AI work with fewer paid GPU-hours.

US$20 million/year spent on GPU time eligible for the improvement

If the customer achieves+1%more completed work per paid GPU-hour
→
The example is worthUS$≈198k/yrless GPU rental spend per year for the same work

Who benefits: Buyer of metered GPU compute.

To turn this into money: The customer must be able to stop paying for the hours released. Fixed commitments or an owned fleet create spare capacity first, not an immediate cash saving.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:
Assume 1% higher sustained throughput on the same workload and hardware, at the same hourly rate, power budget and temperature limits. The entire US$20m example budget is eligible.
Calculation:
US$20,000,000 − (US$20,000,000 ÷ 1.01) ≈ US$198,020/year.
Financial meaning:
Avoided operating spend. A reduction in actual recurring spending, before adoption costs and Carbon fees.
Proof needed:
Measure completed work over full workload cycles, including recovery time. Confirm quality, billing granularity, utilisation and hardware limits.
Technical context:
NVIDIA: Performance Benchmarking ↗. This source supports the mechanism, not these assumed gains or dollar inputs.

Hypothetical example, not a Carbon result or forecast. Deduct Carbon fees and all adoption costs. Customer benefit is not Carbon revenue.

03

Optical AI Links

Design for variation, not perfection.

Industrial use

Screen 3D fiber-to-chip grating designs across wavelength, alignment and fabrication variation.

Impact to prove

More devices meeting the optical specification across actual manufacturing variation.

Illustrative annual customer value

Sell more working optical devices from the same production run.

One million finished-device production starts per year

If the customer achieves+1 ppsaleable-device yield: 90% → 91%
→
The example is worthUS$1m/yradditional annual contribution from 10,000 extra devices sold

Who benefits: Optical-device manufacturer.

To turn this into money: The manufacturer must confirm final device yield and sell the extra units. Passing a coupler test alone does not establish saleable-product yield.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:
Assume improved coupling converts 10,000 otherwise lost devices into saleable units, worth US$100 each after additional finishing, testing and selling costs.
Calculation:
1,000,000 devices/year × (91% − 90%) × US$100/device = US$1,000,000/year.
Financial meaning:
Additional operating contribution. Extra sales less the variable costs of earning them, before fixed overhead and adoption costs.
Proof needed:
Test fabrication and alignment variation, downstream acceptance and reliability. Exclude units already recovered through rework and deduct process-change costs.
Technical context:
imec: Interfacing silicon photonics for high-density co-packaged optics ↗. This source supports the mechanism, not these assumed gains or dollar inputs.

Less charging dwell at independently checked cell-specific constraints.

Illustrative annual customer value

Return vehicles to paid work sooner.

1,000 comparable on-shift charges/day over 300 operating days/year

If the customer achieves−5 mincharging time per event, within the same cell limits
→
The example is worthUS$500k/yradditional annual contribution from productive vehicle time

Who benefits: Time-constrained fleet operator.

To turn this into money: Charging must interrupt revenue-producing work. Overnight or idle-time charging may produce no additional income even if it finishes faster.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:

Calculation:

Financial meaning:
Additional operating contribution. Extra sales less the variable costs of earning them, before fixed overhead and adoption costs.
Proof needed:
Confirm the same starting and target charge, safety and cell-life limits. Measure usable fleet time and deduct charger upgrades, demand charges and any battery-life cost.

05

Precision Motors

Optimize the whole torque curve.

Industrial use

Screen motor geometry and current for smooth torque while retaining required mean torque.

Impact to prove

Lower torque ripple without sacrificing required mean torque.

Illustrative annual customer value

Make more saleable parts by reducing motion-related defects.

Illustrative production application: one million parts started per year

If the customer achieves2% → 1%parts rejected because of motion errors
→
The example is worthUS$200k/yradditional annual contribution from 10,000 recovered parts sold

Who benefits: Precision-manufacturing operator.

To turn this into money: The customer must prove that motor torque variation causes those defects. A reduction in torque ripple is not the same percentage reduction in scrap.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:
Assume smoother motor-driven motion reduces motion-related rejects by one percentage point. Each extra saleable part adds US$20 after remaining variable costs.
Calculation:
1,000,000 parts/year × (2% − 1%) × US$20/part = US$200,000/year.
Financial meaning:
Additional operating contribution. Extra sales less the variable costs of earning them, before fixed overhead and adoption costs.
Proof needed:
Measure motion and final part quality with a tuned controller, at the same mean torque, speed and production conditions. Confirm demand for recovered parts.
Technical context:
Kollmorgen: Cogging Torque vs Torque Ripple ↗. This source supports the mechanism, not these assumed gains or dollar inputs.

Hypothetical example, not a Carbon result or forecast. Deduct Carbon fees and all adoption costs. Customer benefit is not Carbon revenue.

06

Low-Vibration Robotics

Put stiffness where motion needs it.

Industrial use

Compare robot-link and precision-stage geometries under mass, stiffness and vibration constraints.

Impact to prove

Less vibration while meeting mass and stiffness requirements.

Illustrative annual customer value

Produce more on the same automation line.

One line, 4,000 productive hours/year, one accepted part per cycle

If the customer achieves10 → 9.5 stotal machine cycle after reducing vibration settling time
→
The example is worthUS$≈379k/yradditional annual contribution from about 75,789 extra parts sold

Who benefits: Precision-automation operator.

To turn this into money: The line must be cycle-time constrained, with demand and downstream capacity for the extra output. A 25% shorter settling phase means 5% off this total cycle, not 25% more production.

Assumptions, calculation and proof needed
Assumed inputs:
Assume settling falls from 2.0 to 1.5 seconds inside a 10-second cycle; the other 8 seconds stay unchanged. Each extra part adds US$5 after variable costs.
Calculation:
[(4,000 × 3,600 ÷ 9.5) − (4,000 × 3,600 ÷ 10)] × US$5 ≈ US$378,947/year.
Financial meaning:
Additional operating contribution. Extra sales less the variable costs of earning them, before fixed overhead and adoption costs.
Proof needed:
Measure the complete machine cycle at the same positioning tolerance, payload, mass, stiffness and accepted-quality requirements.
Technical context:
Aerotech: Improving machine throughput with ETM ↗. This source supports the mechanism, not these assumed gains or dollar inputs.

Hypothetical example, not a Carbon result or forecast. Deduct Carbon fees and all adoption costs. Customer benefit is not Carbon revenue.

Process more paid samples with the same mixing equipment.

What these dollar figures mean

These are one-year customer scenarios with assumed inputs. The supplied material does not demonstrate these gains. Cost savings require an actual bill or build-cost reduction; extra contribution requires additional sales after their variable costs. The figures are not market forecasts or Carbon revenue.

Deduct data, training, verification, hardware changes, integration, serving, upkeep and Carbon fees to calculate net value. Validate the gain against a strong existing workflow. Do not add overlapping benefits or multiply these examples into an industry total without eligible-customer and adoption evidence. Customer types are targets, not clients; card visuals are concepts.
