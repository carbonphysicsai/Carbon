01 / Your Role in Carbon

You execute the contract. You don’t write it.

A validator runs a versioned scientific contract. You rebuild candidates, apply the registered tests and record what happened. The contract fixes the gates, thresholds and scoring before anyone competes, and changes only by versioned update. That separation between proposing, judging and rule-setting is what makes the evidence worth something to the engineer who ends up relying on it.

Miners

  • Propose reproducible designs
  • Run permitted practice
  • Never see protected cases

Validators

  • Reconstruct selected candidates
  • Execute the registered exam
  • Record reproducible results

The contract

  • Defines the exam before anyone competes
  • Fixes gates, thresholds and scoring
  • Changes only by versioned update

02 / Reconstruct the Candidate

Rebuild it from the design. Record how.

  1. 01

    Bind the design

    Pin the submitted strategy to the approved implementation, the environment and the challenge’s resource limits. Nothing outside that binding runs.

  2. 02

    Use validator-controlled data

    Reconstruction data is yours, not the miner’s. Record every identity needed to reproduce the run: versions, seeds, environment, resources, provenance.

  3. 03

    Reconstruct under the selection policy

    The active, versioned selection policy decides which candidates undergo full reconstruction, so validator compute goes where it changes the answer.

03 / Establish the Reference

The generator makes the cases. The reference answers them.

These are two different authorities. A challenge’s case generator samples the target population. The reference answer method, a qualified analytic solution, a checked numerical solver or another accepted anchor, says what the right answer is, with its own uncertainty and scope. A deterministic generator can still be scientifically wrong if it samples the wrong population or labels cases inaccurately, which is why Carbon qualifies the exam before the exam qualifies anyone.

Case generator

Produces protected cases from the declared population and sampling plan.

Reference method

The qualified anchor each candidate is judged against, with stated uncertainty and scope.

04 / Run the Physics and Robustness Tests

Three questions, in order.

Accuracy

Does it match the reference?

Error against the qualified answer on protected cases.

Physical behaviour

Does it obey the physics?

Mandatory, challenge-specific gates: conservation, boundary behaviour, rollout stability, finite outputs. Fail one and the candidate is out, regardless of accuracy.

Robustness

Does it hold when conditions change?

Registered stress tests inside the declared operating range, on cases the miner has never seen.

Participants know what competence is required. They never know which draws will decide their score. That asymmetry is what keeps the exam honest as agents get better at optimising against it.

05 / Score, Compare and Record

Gates before rank. Evidence before reward.

  1. 01

    Apply the mandatory gates

    Only admissible candidates are ranked. Invalid, infrastructure-failed, inadmissible and indeterminate outcomes are recorded as exactly that, and kept distinct from one another.

  2. 02

    Score and compare

    The challenge’s scoring contract ranks eligible candidates. A challenger is compared with the incumbent on the same fresh promotion exam, and the comparison returns superior, not superior or indeterminate.

  3. 03

    Record and settle

    Every run leaves a reproducibility record. Only a verified frontier advance creates a reward entitlement, and settlement is transported by the current incentive mechanism, separate from the scientific record.

Admissibility precedes ranking. A candidate is judged only against an exam Carbon has qualified first.

Rank nominates. Evidence qualifies. A competition winner is a nomination, not a qualified product.
