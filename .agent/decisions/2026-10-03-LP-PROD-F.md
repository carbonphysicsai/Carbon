## 2026-10-03 — LP-PROD-F: the Control Center redraws only on change, holds a request only while its outcome is unknown, and says what the record says

**Authority.** Delegated engineering decision (executor, slice F of the
Launchpad production work list), within OWNER-LAUNCHPAD-PROD-01 (recorded by
slice A): the owner asked that the miner front end, "our entire workforce
facing application", be zero-friction, production-level and state of the art,
and approved the work list on 2026-10-03 ("approve. you launch this now"). It
changes how the page draws and reports. It changes no scientific value, gate
or tolerance, no economics or default spend, no chain write, no exposure of a
host, and no authority boundary: the signer still holds every key, miner
compute stays link-only, and nothing hidden reaches the page.

**Why it is needed.** A live audit of `scripts/dev/miner_launchpad` at
`dff575f` found the page fighting its own users:
- every 1.5 s refresh rebuilt the launch wizard's steps, Model and Compute
  choices and review, the campaign lists, and (on any change in the view) the
  whole campaign page. A click pressed while a region was rebuilt was lost
  (the Next button needed retries, live), and an agent's element references
  went stale;
- the Model step could not be passed for a provider whose adapter lists no
  models (Chutes, Anthropic, the OpenAI-compatible adapters), although the
  runner already launches with the profile's `model_selection`;
- a pending launch was kept after every failure, including a definite
  refusal, and its retry resent the stale body, so `research_review_changed`
  repeated forever; there was no way to let it go;
- a submit answered 200 was announced as "Submitted ... The independent
  result appears below" while the controller had only started it;
- the Live tab hid Reconcile, and a campaign whose view could not be read
  had no controls at all;
- the Tools tab's freeze and submit sent a new idempotency key per click (a
  retry after a timeout met its own first attempt as `campaign_busy`), and
  wrote their answer as 400 characters of JSON into a panel the same click
  had just closed;
- budgets were typed in nanodollars and milliseconds; agent and compute
  choices had different names on different pages; `#campaigns/<id>/overview`
  links named tabs the research surface does not have; Connections showed a
  placeholder MCP command.

**Decision.**

1. *Live regions.* A region is redrawn only when the data it shows changed
   (a key per region; the campaign page is a fixed frame of parts, each with
   its own key; the wizard's step list and choices are built once and patched
   in place). A region is never rebuilt while it holds the focused form
   control or an unsent typed edit (a field a person or agent typed into,
   marked by its own `input` event; a draft-backed journey field restores
   itself and does not hold). During a background refresh it is also left
   alone while a pointer is pressed in it, and while a pointer rests on one
   of its controls for at most 3 s (`HOVER_HOLD_MS`): `:hover` matches every
   ancestor of the element under the pointer (a whole campaign card), and an
   agent leaves its pointer where it last clicked, so a resting pointer must
   never keep a state it is watching for stale. It is redrawn on the first
   refresh after the hold ends. A redraw the person caused (their own click)
   is drawn at once. Attributes and text are set only when they differ.
2. *Refusal and unknown outcome.* A request was refused only when the
   controller answered 4xx with its own code. A network failure, a timeout, a
   5xx, an unreadable answer, and the controller's catch-all for an
   unexpected failure (`research_reconciliation_required`, also `request_failed`)
   leave the outcome unknown.
3. *Pending launch.* The launch is held in this tab's session storage only
   while its outcome is unknown, with its idempotency key. A retry always
   sends the wizard's current choices (and the review the controller states
   now) under the held key, so a launch already recorded is replayed or
   refused as `research_launch_replay_conflict`, never started twice. The key
   is released on success; on a refusal when no earlier attempt under it is
   unknown; on `research_launch_replay_conflict` (the earlier campaign stands
   and is listed); or by Discard. A refusal after an unknown attempt keeps
   the key. The rehearsal launch follows the same rule and also gets Discard.
4. *Keyed operations.* The journey's practice, freeze and submit and the
   Tools tab's freeze and submit go through one helper that holds one key per
   operation and campaign. (The Tools tab's "Practice this recipe" is the
   research tools' own `start_research_task`, with a business id per click,
   as before; it is not keyed here.) The held keys are one map in session
   storage under the existing key `carbon.launchpad.pending-operation.v1`; a
   single held request written by the page before this change is read as an
   unknown one. Each held key carries the mark of the campaign's record when
   it was first sent (submitted epochs, the frozen flag, experiments,
   refusals, `last_refusal`, state). A held key is released when the
   controller answers it; on a refusal with no earlier attempt under it
   outstanding; on `operation_replay_conflict` (the key is spent and nothing
   new ran); by Discard (in the journey and the Tools tab); and, on any
   refresh while it is not in flight, when the record shows how it ended or
   that it no longer applies: its effect (a new submitted epoch, a frozen
   candidate, a new experiment), a refusal recorded since (one slice C names
   for another operation does not count), an interruption, the campaign
   ending, or (for submit) its frozen candidate gone. A key is therefore never
   carried into the next epoch's request: before this, a submit whose answer
   was lost but which ran left its key held, and the next candidate's submit
   reused it and was replayed, so nothing was dispatched. A retry with the
   same values reuses the key; a retry with different values (an edited
   hypothesis or reason) is sent as a new request under a new key, and the
   page says so (if the earlier one started, it ran too) instead of claiming
   it is the same request.
5. *Started is not done.* The page says an operation started when the
   controller answers it, and says it is done only when the campaign's own
   record shows it: a new submitted epoch for submit ("Submitted for the
   DEVELOPMENT comparison. The independent result appears below." appears
   only then), a frozen candidate for freeze, a new experiment for practice.
   A new `last_refusal` (for this operation, when it names one) or refusal
   note is shown as not done, with its next action. With no change of record
   a minute after the work ended, it says so rather than guessing. A retry
   answered for an earlier unknown attempt is read against the mark of its
   first send, so a replay of work that already finished reads as done.
6. *The campaign's last refusal.* Slice C's `last_refusal` `{code,
   next_action, at, operation, kind}` is read from the campaign view or the
   campaign record, shown above the campaign's header with what to do (as
   refused, interrupted or paused, and the operation it was for when named)
   and, for a code this page can fix (signer, registration, model provider,
   profile), the link to fix it; and as one line on its campaign card. A
   missing or malformed field shows nothing.
7. *Recovery.* When the record carries slice C's `recovery` actions
   (`[{action, operation}]`, empty when nothing is needed), those are what
   the page offers (first among the Live tab's controls, on the Overview's
   card, and on the recovery line when the campaign view cannot be read),
   with a hint that names them; PAUSED, the miner's own choice, gets no
   alarm. Without it (a controller before slice C), the page reads the
   state: PAUSE_REQUESTED, RECONCILIATION_REQUIRED and INTERRUPTED get a
   one-line hint and Reconcile; QUEUED with no frozen record gets "never
   started here" only once it has stayed so for 30 s as this page has seen
   it, since a fresh launch is QUEUED until its run thread creates its ledger.
   When the campaign view cannot be read, the campaign's own state still
   offers Stop, Resume, Pause, Export and Reconcile.
8. *Setup's model.* The capability document carries `model.setup_choice`
   (only the profile's `model_selection` provider and model ids; never its
   endpoint, price or key file) and lists that model under its provider,
   marked `from_setup`, when the adapter does not list it. The wizard
   preselects it; with no provider chosen the Model step passes when setup's
   provider is available, and the launch then names none and the runner uses
   setup's choice, as it already did. A launch never names a model the page
   has not listed.
9. *Human units.* Ceilings are typed in USD for `provider_nanodollars`,
   minutes for `numerical_milliseconds` and MB for `retained_bytes`, and sent
   as the ledger's whole numbers; counts stay counts. Conversion is exact
   decimal arithmetic on the whole number, never floating point: a typed cap
   is rounded down, never above what was typed; a stored cap is shown
   exactly (USD to the nanodollar, MB to the byte; minutes rounded up in the
   fifth place, so the text reads back as the same milliseconds); and a
   ceiling field not typed into since it was filled keeps its exact stored
   value when any other limit is edited. Summaries show money with at least
   two places and every finer digit (a non-zero cap never reads as $0.00),
   and worker time as hours, minutes and seconds. The usage tables (Logs, and
   the fallback page's metrics) show every column, limit, reported, reserved
   and uncertain, in those same units. The elapsed limit stays in seconds
   (already a human unit, and pinned by the browser smoke), with minutes or
   hours beside it. Saved wizard choices and templates keep the ledger's
   units, so nothing stored changes meaning.
10. *Names.* Choices this document shares with setup are named as setup names
    them (`Carbon's autonomous research agent`, `Your own agent, over MCP`,
    `This machine (CPU)` or `(your GPU)`, `Your own remote machine or
    container`), held to `environment_setup.choices` by test. The Compute
    page's two route headings (`This machine`, `A GPU you run elsewhere`) are
    headings, not choices, and keep their LINKONLY-D10 names.
11. *Links.* A campaign's tabs are the research surface's. `overview`,
    `metrics`, `journal`, `notes`, `messages` and `outcomes` (and a missing
    tab) open the tab they meant, and the address is rewritten in place to
    its name; the page's own links now name `live`.
12. *The MCP command.* With a runner profile loaded, Connections shows the
    command `carbon.miner_mcp.agent_connection.connection_instructions` builds
    for that profile's path, made absolute against the controller's working
    directory (the miner's MCP client starts the server from its own), shell-
    quoted, and its client configuration; without one, the placeholder, with
    where the path comes from. Naming the path loads nothing: the server
    verifies the profile when it starts.
13. *Sending the worker.* Until the controller answers (up to an hour), the
    page shows how long it has been going, beside an indeterminate progress
    bar; it never sends twice at once. A send that does not go through leaves
    its button ready again.

**Tests.** `tests/cpu/test_control_center_live_page.py` builds the documents
from the real capability, options, setup and campaign-view code and runs
`tests/cpu/control_center_page_check.cjs`: 30 scenarios that drive index.html
and its four scripts through a small DOM (`tests/cpu/control_center_dom.cjs`)
on a fake clock, against a scripted controller, when Node is installed (as in
CI). The first 22 fail against the page at `origin/main` (all but the
harmless-when-absent refusal check); the 8 added after review (the bounded
hover hold, slice C's recovery actions, the exact cap round trip, the usage
units, a lost submit that ran, a retry answered after its work finished, an
edited retry and Discard, and a failed send) and the changed never-started
grace fail against the page before the review fixes. Those are their
specimens. The same file holds the capability document's new fields to
their sources.

**Not decided here.** Whether `last_refusal` is written, when it is cleared
and what codes it carries, and what `recovery` offers in which state, are
slice C's contract; this page only renders them, and falls back to its own
reading of the state when they are absent.
