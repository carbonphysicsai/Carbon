1. Measure the nonlinearity first: the peak change between constant k(25 °C) and k(T) at the hot corners. If it's only a few U, add a stronger nonseparable nonlinearity (the a2 h(T) onset curve, or a sourced TIM k(T)), as a proposal to the owner.
2. The closed-form gate's adversary is the strongest cheap method: structured superposition (Duhamel step response per patch, modes interpolated over h and TIM) plus a k(T) correction, AND the client's "~10 solver runs plus fit". STOP if either comes within the crown margin of the oracle on any target. The gate criterion is owner-signed.
3. Ranges: parameterise start as coolant + offset ≥ 0 (or record start-below-coolant as intended); T7 needs a minimum share of nonzero time-above-90 in every context; T8 needs ≥ 2 feasible designs in every context; the worst-corner peak stays inside the 20–150 °C tables.
4. The new constant-property bounds law is proved or demoted to a checked diagnostic before it becomes a gate.
5. Fix the TRAIN/gate order (solve TRAIN or a probe TRAIN before the gate); add a separate test of the piecewise-linear table path (MMS uses a smooth k(T)); pin the nonlinear-iteration tolerance; the schedule is about 2 weeks.
6. TRAIN publishes scalar outputs only, never full T(t) traces. The T8 hot context is drawn per rotation from a registered hot band. The probe pool is never reused as a graded pool, and shell targets use the shell U.
