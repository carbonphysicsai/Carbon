01 · The Pitch

A subnet that sells trained models, not answered requests

Miners propose training strategies (backbone choice, loss terms, curriculum) for a physics challenge. Validators run the official train and eval of that exact strategy against real-time-generated data and battle-test it against real physics. The output is a Model Card, and the growing set of those is the product: a licensable model zoo for engineering teams, not a live answer to a submitted request.

Constraint 01

Validator compute is the scarce resource

The validator trains and evaluates every submission from scratch. That's the real cost center now, not a separate verification step layered on top.

Constraint 02

Registration is a sunk cost

Current dynamic registration burn runs $1.5–2.5M+, non-refundable, and roughly doubles with each new subnet.

Constraint 03

The hardware burden is asymmetric

A miner submits a JSON strategy: no GPU required at all. The validator needs real training capacity. That's the opposite shape from a typical GPU-mining subnet.

02 · Prior Art

Carbon isn't the first physics subnet

Subnet 25 (Mainframe) solved the same trust problem for protein folding, and just partnered with Rowan Scientific to generate DFT data for neural network potentials. Same pitch, one science over.

04 · Why This Can Work

The validator is the only one who ever runs it

A miner can propose anything, but they never touch the data or the training run. The validator executes the exact submitted strategy itself, on data the miner couldn't have seen coming. That's what makes the result trustworthy, not a cheap post-hoc check.

Fresh seeded draws

Every train and eval set is generated in real time from a qualified procedural generator, seed-triggered per run. There's no static answer key to memorize or reverse-engineer.

Fail-closed hard gates

Finite output, mass conservation, energy stability, short-rollout stability: any mandatory gate failing zeroes the score outright. No partial credit for a strategy that looks close.

A miner who proposes a strategy that only works on data they've seen has nowhere to hide: the validator's own execution on unseen draws is the entire test.
05 · Miner Side

Miners propose, they don't compute

A strategy is a menu selection plus a recipe: which backbone, which loss terms, what curriculum. The miner never trains it themselves, so mining requires no GPU at all, just a well-reasoned JSON submission and a small evaluation fee.

FNOGINOWNO GNOTransolverGAOT PhysicsNeMo-FNOPINO

This is the backbone menu already live in code (ALLOWED_BACKBONES), plus GAOT and GNO for geometries that outgrow a regular grid. Full detail on the knobs around each choice is next.

Zero hardware requirement for mining is a real structural difference from typical Bittensor subnets, where miners themselves bear the GPU cost. Here that cost concentrates entirely on the validator.
06 · Strategy Schema

Every new knob earns its place on a scored leg

Scoring weights physics 0.40, robustness 0.35, accuracy 0.25 (detail on the Incentives slide), with accuracy deliberately lowest so memorization can't dominate. A knob only belongs in the schema if it gives a miner a real lever on one of those legs.

New loss termMovesWhy it's there
noise_robustness Robustness leg (0.35, highest-weighted) Penalizes output sensitivity to small input/IC perturbations, directly targeting the stress categories
rollout_consistency short_rollout hard gate Penalizes drift under autoregressive rollout vs. single-step prediction
spectral_matching Physics leg Counters FNO's known high-wavenumber spectral bias
sobolev_penalty Physics leg Matches spatial derivatives, not just field values: gradients matter for elasticity and NS
smoothness_tv Physics leg Standard fix for Gibbs-like oscillation near shocks (Burgers)
Phase 0 priority: noise_robustness and rollout_consistency first. Both map onto the two highest-weighted scoring elements and need no new generator or reference-solver work to implement.
07 · Strategy Schema, cont.

Curriculum, and what stays off-limits

Curriculum · new, not yet implemented

Named by the team as a wanted knob but absent from the live schema. Proposed: viscosity_anneal trains on smooth high-viscosity draws first, then anneals toward the shock-forming regime. loss_weight_schedule starts data-fit dominant, then ramps physics weight up, the standard fix for physics losses blocking early convergence.

Explicitly off-limits to miners

No control over seeds: fresh validator-generated draws are the entire anti-gaming invariant. No visibility into gate thresholds, Score Pack weights, or eval/stress data. What's exposed is the recipe, never the answer key.

Also proposed: a regularization block (dropout, normalization, activation, weight init, EMA) and a compute_tier field tying the "pay for an edge" idea to a concrete, still hard-capped budget ceiling. Backbone list needs reconciling too: fold TFNO into an FNO factorization option rather than a separate backbone name, since it's the same architecture, not a different one.
08 · Framework & Hardware

One codebase, two backends, used differently on each side

Miner side: no hardware decision at all

A strategy names a backbone (fno, transolver, ...), it never specifies GPU or TPU. That choice doesn't exist on the miner's side to make, because the miner never runs the model. Whatever the strategy asks for, the validator is the one who executes it.

Validator side: TPU is the clearer win

The validator has to train and evaluate every submitted strategy on real-time-generated data fast enough to keep up with submission traffic. pmap/pjit batching across a TPU pod scales that well, and FNO-style spectral convolutions (FFT + dense matmul) map cleanly onto the MXU. The nearest public comparable, NOVA (SN68), runs its validator-side model on 2× GPUs with >48GB VRAM each, verified directly against its README rather than a secondhand estimate.

Recommendation: JAX everywhere as the framework choice. TPU where the validator needs batch throughput. Miners need no GPU at all, they only submit a strategy.
09 · Distributed Training, Resolved

Validator-side parallelism, not cross-miner training

An earlier version of this design explored an orchestrator that split one shared model's training across many miners, pipeline-parallel, IOTA-style. That doesn't match the confirmed mechanism: one strategy in, one validator trains it, one Model Card out. There's no step where multiple miners' compute combines into a single job.

What's actually needed

Validator-side execution capacity

If a submitted strategy specifies a large model, the validator's own training infrastructure needs to run it, potentially across its own multi-GPU box (standard sharding, e.g. ZeRO/FSDP), not across the network.

What's not needed, for now

An orchestrator role

SN9/IOTA-style cross-miner pipeline parallelism stays on the shelf unless the product direction moves toward one large shared foundation model instead of many strategy-scoped ones.

Keeping this slide rather than deleting it on purpose: it's a real design decision we made and then walked back once the mechanism was confirmed, worth having on record so it doesn't get re-litigated from scratch later.
10 · The Data Flywheel

Every run is a data point, not just a result

Open path · the Model Zoo

Model Cards accumulate into a licensable, growing catalog. This is the direct product: engineering teams get fast surrogates for design exploration, real-time control, and agentic solving.

Specialist path · sponsored hardening

The knowledge graph of what's working and what isn't feeds a deliberately more rigorous re-validation pass, building specialist models for industry sponsors targeting their own physics envelope, without exposing their proprietary data.

Mining is expected to be agentic. The miner feedback loop, next up after this deck, is the mechanism that lets agents iterate locally on the accumulated intelligence without paying the submission fee every time.
11 · Incentives

Gate first, then score, then shape the curve

The real Score Pack formula, not an approximation of it.

gate_failed = any mandatory gate fails # finite, mass_conservation, energy_stability, short_rollout S_combined = 0 # if gate_failed: fail-closed, no partial credit S_combined = 0.40·physics + 0.35·robustness + 0.25·accuracy # otherwise weight = reward_curve(S_combined) # Top-K / 80-20, or continuous (open)

Accuracy is deliberately the lowest-weighted leg, so pure memorization can't dominate emissions. SN25 uses Top-K, winner-take-most: sharper competition, higher variance, discourages small miners. Continuous scoring is gentler but slower to punish mediocre strategies. Still open which shape Carbon uses.
