# 2. Investor-readable problem

Engineering organizations increasingly want fast physical models for optimization, control, digital twins, uncertainty studies, and agentic engineering. The commercial bottleneck is not only model inference speed. It is the expensive and fragmented process of:

- deciding what fast-model method to use;
- obtaining enough trustworthy evidence to rely on it;
- identifying failure regimes;
- independently reproducing results;
- qualifying a specific model for a job;
- maintaining that evidence as the model and operating context change.

Carbon's business thesis is:

> **Build the discovery, independent-evidence, qualification, and lifecycle infrastructure around fast physical models, then use an open research network to scale the parts of R&D/search that benefit from external competition.**

The customer buys an engineering outcome and evidence package. The scientific protocol remains underneath the product rather than becoming the sales pitch itself.

# 15. Alpha investor framing

Do not present Alpha as equity, revenue share, or guaranteed financial beneficiary of OpCo revenue.

Current Bittensor economics use subnet-specific Alpha tokens and TAO/Alpha pools; subnet emission share depends on market-valued subnet price mechanics, while miners/validators/owners receive subnet Alpha according to network rules. Carbon should therefore make **commercially useful network activity** the bridge between company adoption and network utility rather than claim automatic pass-through from invoices to token holders.

Investor-safe company framing:

> **The company monetizes enterprise products. The subnet supplies a scalable external research/evaluation economy. As commercial workloads increasingly use the subnet, the network has more real economic utility; token economics remain governed by Bittensor rather than by an OpCo revenue promise.**

Any buyback, revenue-share, token purchase obligation, financial return promise, or similar mechanism requires separate legal/economic review.

# 18. Investor objection gauntlet

## “This is a consulting business.”

Risk is real. Countermeasure is measurable productization: reusable adapters, standardized Audits, software workflow, recurring lifecycle, platform/API, network supply leverage, rising revenue per technical FTE and gross margin.

## “The market is too niche.”

Do not argue from a speculative TAM number. Demonstrate that engineering software is a multi-billion-dollar recurring category and prove a narrow high-value wedge across several physics-intensive industries. Expand only after customer evidence.

## “Why won't Ansys/Siemens/Dassault build this?”

They can build parts. Carbon's differentiation must be independent evidence, cross-tool/model neutrality, open external search supply, evidence lineage, and qualification infrastructure that can complement rather than require displacement of incumbent workflows.

## “Why decentralize?”

Do not defend decentralization ideologically. Prove that the network provides better search diversity, economics, or access to external intelligence than centralized staffing for eligible programs.

## “The token is a distraction.”

It will be if placed in front of the buyer. Enterprise UX remains conventional. Investor case stands on OpCo economics; network utility is separately measured.

## “Enterprise sales will take forever.”

Use the Evidence Audit as a bounded paid pilot with a clear technical buyer and deliverable. Build partner/OEM channels later. Avoid requiring the customer to adopt a company-wide platform first.

## “Customer data/IP will block the network.”

Private Challenge/customer-hosted truth/VPC architecture is a major implementation priority. Until qualified, private engagements may remain off-chain/off-network while the company still earns revenue.

## “Where is the moat if customers keep their data?”

Moat is not raw customer data alone: evidence protocols, qualified workflows, adapters, rights-permitted aggregates, qualification registry, network supply, customer integrations, and later validated decision intelligence.

## “What if your scientific system finds no winner?”

That is a valid result. Base program revenue pays for the evidence/search program; participant success rewards remain separate. Carbon must not depend on fabricating winners.

## “How does this become $100M+ revenue?”

Not through Audits alone. The architecture requires platform/OEM/API, recurring lifecycle, high-value discovery programs, licenses, and network marketplace scale. Audits are the customer-acquisition wedge.

# 19. Business stop-ships

Investor/commercial architecture should be reconsidered if evidence shows:

1. customers do not value independent evidence enough to pay material pilot fees;
2. Audit projects cannot be standardized and remain permanently bespoke;
3. follow-on attach rate is negligible;
4. security/private deployment costs overwhelm contract value;
5. gross margin does not improve with repeated delivery;
6. enterprise platform demand fails to emerge from repeat customers;
7. the network does not create useful search/economic leverage;
8. rights/privacy prevent any compounding advantage;
9. qualification/lifecycle creates no recurring buyer need;
10. Carbon must replace core CAE workflows rather than integrate with them to win.

These are falsifiable business hypotheses, not rhetorical objections.
