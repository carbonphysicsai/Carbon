# 1. Executive result

The mapped commercial opportunities are coherent as a portfolio, but they should not be sold with one generic motion.

The opportunities divide into four sales archetypes:

```text
A. DIAGNOSTIC / EVIDENCE SALE
   Evidence Audit

B. R&D / PROGRAM SALE
   Challenge Design
   Sponsored Discovery
   Model Development

C. QUALIFICATION / LIFECYCLE SALE
   Qualified Model
   Lifecycle / Requalification

D. PLATFORM / CHANNEL SALE
   Enterprise Evidence Platform
   OEM / Evidence API
   Frontier Market
   Physics Intelligence (later)
```

The best commercial sequencing remains:

```text
LAND WITH EVIDENCE
      ↓
EXPAND INTO DISCOVERY / REMEDIATION
      ↓
QUALIFY / DEPLOY
      ↓
RECUR THROUGH LIFECYCLE
      ↓
CONVERT REPEATED USE INTO PLATFORM / API
      ↓
SCALE DISCOVERY THROUGH THE NETWORK
      ↓
MONETIZE INTELLIGENCE ONLY WHEN EARNED
```

Not every customer follows this chain, but this is the default account-development architecture.

# 2. Common qualification frame

Before selling any Carbon product, qualify these six dimensions:

1. **Decision value** — what engineering, product, or economic decision depends on the model?
2. **Existing pain** — credibility, search difficulty, latency, robustness, qualification, integration, or lifecycle?
3. **Truth access** — solver, dataset, experiment, third-party source, or customer-hosted truth?
4. **Authority owner** — who can approve the technical claim and who owns budget?
5. **Security / rights** — what can leave the customer environment, what can be reused, what is confidential?
6. **Commercial outcome** — audit report, better method, qualified artifact, platform capability, or recurring lifecycle service?

A lead that cannot answer these is not proposal-ready.

# 3. Opportunity A — Carbon Evidence Audit

## Ideal customer profile

Organizations already building or deploying fast physics models, surrogates, reduced models, operator-learning systems, or digital-twin components.

Best early sectors:
- aerospace / space / defense;
- energy / turbomachinery / industrial physics;
- engineering software / simulation vendors;
- advanced manufacturing / mobility where surrogate models already exist.

## Buyer / champion / budget

**Champion:** SciML lead, simulation lead, digital-twin lead, model V&V lead.  
**Economic buyer:** VP/Head of Engineering, Chief Engineer, CTO, simulation software GM, R&D director.  
**Budget source:** R&D, digital engineering, simulation, verification/validation, product engineering.

## Trigger events

- model is approaching deployment;
- internal disagreement about trust/robustness;
- customer/regulator/engineering authority requests evidence;
- surrogate fails in edge cases;
- optimization loop exploits model error;
- team wants independent diligence before larger investment;
- acquisition/partnership diligence on a physics-AI model.

## Discovery questions

- What model do you already have?
- What decision/job is it supposed to support?
- Where do you currently not trust it?
- What truth source exists?
- What would a failed audit change?
- Who needs to sign off on the model?
- What evidence do they currently lack?
- What is the cost of a false-positive model decision?
- Is the model used descriptively, in optimization, control, or autonomous workflows?
- What confidentiality constraints apply?

## Minimum proof required to sell

- one end-to-end Carbon-authored evaluation on a representative model;
- evidence package understandable to a technical reviewer;
- clear separation between model failure and infrastructure failure;
- ability to show a failure mode the customer's normal metric set missed, or independently confirm bounded strength.

## Commercial package

```text
Evidence Audit Base
+ Truth/Adapter Integration
+ finite Evaluation Capacity
+ optional Private/VPC/On-Prem premium
+ optional Remediation/Qualification follow-on
```

## Pricing unit

Fixed scoped package with bounded truth/evaluation budget. Overage/change-order rules for additional models, regimes, truth calls, adapters, or private deployment.

## Objections

**"We can test it ourselves."**  
Response: Carbon must demonstrate independent evaluation, protected evidence, qualified exam design, repeatability, and reduced internal bias / reviewer burden.

**"This is consulting."**  
Response: deliverable is a standardized evidence package backed by reusable evaluation infrastructure, not just advice.

**"What if it fails?"**  
Response: a scientifically honest negative result is successful delivery.

## Expansion

```text
Audit failure -> Remediation / Sponsored Discovery
Audit promising -> Product Qualification
Multiple models -> Enterprise Evidence Platform
Deployment -> Lifecycle / Requalification
```

## Recurrence

High once customer has multiple model versions, programs, or changing contexts of use.

## Network role

Initially optional. Audit can be off-chain/private. Later, comparative evaluation, external replications, or sponsored remediation may become network-eligible.

## Verdict

**BEST FIRST PRODUCT.** Highest near-term sellability relative to implementation dependency.

# 4. Opportunity B — Challenge Design / Feasibility

## ICP

Teams that know the engineering problem but do not yet know how to convert it into a credible fast-model research program.

## Buyer

R&D director, simulation leader, digital-engineering leader, innovation program manager, technical founder, government research program manager.

## Trigger

- expensive solver workload;
- interest in surrogate/fast-model development;
- internal teams disagree about benchmark definition;
- sponsor wants to launch a research competition;
- customer needs to scope a digital-twin/model-acceleration program.

## Discovery questions

- What exact job would a fast model accelerate?
- What are required inputs/outputs?
- What operating population matters?
- What truth sources are available?
- What failure would make the model unusable?
- Is the purpose discovery, deployment, qualification, or research publication?
- How much compute/truth budget is available?

## Deliverables

- feasibility assessment;
- candidate input/output contract;
- provisional population/envelope;
- truth-access plan;
- evidence/measurement plan;
- estimated delivery architecture;
- decision: proceed / narrow / blocked.

## Pricing

Fixed-fee technical design sprint, potentially credited partially toward a larger engagement.

## Objection

**"Why pay to define the problem?"**  
Because poorly authored tasks can make expensive search scientifically meaningless; Carbon's Burgers work is internal evidence that this failure mode is real.

## Expansion

Natural feeder into Sponsored Discovery, Evidence Audit, Model Development, or Qualification.

## Network role

Off-chain by default. May produce future network Challenge specifications.

## Verdict

**STRONG LOW-RISK ENTRY PRODUCT**, especially when customer is not yet ready for a full Audit or Discovery Program.

# 5. Opportunity C — Carbon Sponsored Discovery Program

## ICP

Organizations with a high-value physical modeling problem, usable truth path, and willingness to fund external competitive R&D.

## Buyer

VP Engineering, R&D director, simulation/digital-engineering leader, government program manager, CAE product GM.

## Trigger

- internal model-development plateau;
- high cost of repeated simulation;
- desire to compare methods beyond internal team expertise;
- explicit performance frontier or bounty-worthy objective;
- need for faster research iteration.

## Discovery questions

- What baseline must be beaten?
- What scientific improvement matters commercially?
- What is the value of a genuine advance?
- What can be exposed to participants?
- What rights are required in winning methods/artifacts?
- What happens if no one wins?
- What sponsor reward budget is acceptable?
- Can the truth source support protected evaluation at sufficient scale?

## Minimum proof required

- one scientifically credible Challenge;
- multiple real strategies/miners/agents can produce distinguishable outcomes;
- valid frontier-promotion mechanism;
- sponsor reward semantics independent of scientific outcome manipulation;
- a clear no-winner commercial state.

## Commercial package

```text
Challenge Authoring
+ Truth Integration
+ Program Platform Fee
+ Evaluation Budget
+ Sponsor Reward Pool
+ Reward Administration Fee
+ optional Development / Qualification / Deployment
```

## Pricing

Program fee + finite evaluation/compute capacity + separately funded reward pool + optional outcome-based upside.

Do not make Carbon's entire fee contingent on a winner.

## Objections

**"Why expose this to outsiders?"**  
Private/abstracted Challenge modes and rights controls must exist.

**"Why not hire more researchers?"**  
Carbon must prove broader search diversity, faster frontier discovery, or lower marginal R&D cost.

**"What if nobody improves the baseline?"**  
That is a valid research result; unused sponsor reward follows prospective policy.

## Expansion

Winner/candidate -> Qualified Model Program -> deployment -> lifecycle. Repeated sponsor -> Enterprise Platform / Frontier Market.

## Recurrence

Medium-to-high for organizations with recurring modeling portfolios.

## Network role

**CORE NETWORK-DIFFERENTIATED PRODUCT.** Long-term should be network-required for the open/sponsored marketplace version.

## Verdict

**BEST SECOND FLAGSHIP PRODUCT.** Requires more operational maturity than Audit but demonstrates the distinctive Carbon thesis.

# 6. Opportunity D — Carbon Model Development Program

## ICP

Customers who want an actual fast model delivered, not only evidence or search.

## Buyer

Engineering program lead, simulation lead, CTO, product team, digital-twin leader.

## Trigger

- no viable fast model exists;
- existing model failed Audit;
- Discovery Program identified a strong method;
- customer needs delivery responsibility concentrated in one vendor.

## Discovery questions

- What exact artifact must be delivered?
- What deployment target?
- Who owns the construction method and final artifact?
- Is the customer buying a candidate or qualified product?
- What latency/resource constraints apply?
- How will integration acceptance be measured?

## Deliverables

- candidate construction method;
- reconstructed artifact;
- Model Card / evidence lineage;
- deployment package as scoped;
- optional Product Qualification.

## Pricing

Milestone/project fee + adapter/integration costs + optional license/support. Avoid unlimited iteration commitments.

## Objection

**"Why not just hire an ML shop?"**  
Carbon differentiates through independent judge, external search supply, physics evidence, and qualification path.

## Expansion

Qualification, deployment, support, lifecycle, additional models.

## Recurrence

Moderate. Stronger when connected to qualification/lifecycle.

## Network role

Network-eligible when discovery/construction search can use the subnet; customer-specific final engineering may remain OpCo-controlled.

## Verdict

**HIGH CONTRACT VALUE, LOWER INITIAL MARGIN.** Important expansion product, not ideal first wedge.

# 7. Opportunity E — Carbon Qualified Model Program

## ICP

Customers with a specific artifact/system nearing engineering use and needing bounded evidence beyond leaderboard performance.

## Buyer

Chief Engineer, model V&V authority, program engineering lead, safety/reliability engineering, product GM.

## Trigger

- candidate selected for deployment;
- internal gate to release/use;
- customer procurement requires formal diligence;
- model change requires requalification.

## Discovery questions

- What exact context of use?
- What decisions are authorized?
- What answerability/escalation behavior is required?
- What product-level failure modes matter?
- What hardware/runtime is in scope?
- What customer V&V/regulatory process must receive the evidence?

## Deliverables

- Product Battery Record;
- Qualification Record;
- exact artifact/runtime identity;
- limitations and context-of-use statement;
- answerability/escalation policy;
- requalification triggers;
- diligence bundle.

## Pricing

Qualification fee based on evidence burden, artifact/system complexity, truth cost, deployment environment, and review requirements.

## Objections

**"Is this certification?"**  
No universal regulatory claim. Carbon provides bounded evidence and qualification under a defined context.

**"Why repeat tests after the subnet?"**  
Search and product use are different questions; product qualification is intentionally deeper and artifact-specific.

## Expansion

Deployment integration, support, lifecycle/requalification, portfolio/router qualification.

## Recurrence

**HIGH**, because material artifact/context changes naturally trigger new evidence requirements.

## Network role

Separate product plane by default. Network may nominate candidates and provide evidence inputs but does not automatically issue product qualification.

## Verdict

**MAJOR REVENUE + RETENTION ENGINE**, likely a strategic differentiator once productized.

# 8. Opportunity F — Carbon Model Lifecycle / Requalification

## ICP

Organizations operating multiple fast physical models in production or repeated engineering programs.

## Buyer

Model governance owner, digital engineering director, platform owner, product engineering, V&V organization.

## Trigger

- model updates;
- new data;
- new hardware;
- envelope expansion;
- solver/reference change;
- deployment drift;
- new downstream use.

## Discovery questions

- How many models/versions are active?
- How often do they change?
- Who tracks qualification state today?
- What happens when a model leaves its authorized envelope?
- What evidence is required after a release?
- Are there recurring review cycles?

## Deliverables

- model/evidence registry;
- change intake;
- requalification workflow;
- qualification-state dashboard/API;
- drift/escalation hooks;
- periodic evidence updates;
- audit trail.

## Pricing

Annual subscription / platform fee + per-model/per-requalification usage + support/SLA.

## Objection

**"This sounds like governance software."**  
Correct partly; the differentiated value is that the governance state is linked to real Carbon evaluation/qualification evidence, not manually entered compliance metadata only.

## Expansion

Enterprise Evidence Platform, API/OEM, additional departments, portfolio/router governance.

## Recurrence

**VERY HIGH** if embedded in release processes.

## Network role

Mostly OpCo/platform. Network may execute new evaluations/research triggered by lifecycle changes.

## Verdict

**BEST RECURRING ATTACH PRODUCT.** Critical to improving revenue quality and retention.

# 9. Opportunity G — Enterprise Evidence Platform

## ICP

Large organizations repeatedly building/evaluating many physical models across teams and programs.

## Buyer

VP Digital Engineering, simulation platform owner, enterprise AI platform owner, engineering software GM, CIO/CTO for technical computing.

## Trigger

- multiple Audit/Qualification engagements;
- internal model proliferation;
- desire to standardize evidence workflows;
- need for private/VPC/on-prem governance.

## Discovery questions

- How many models/teams/programs?
- What existing model registry exists?
- What evaluation/qualification workflows repeat?
- Which truth sources are standard?
- What SSO/security/data-residency requirements exist?
- Which teams need API access?
- Is there an internal chargeback or usage model?

## Deliverables

- enterprise workspace;
- Challenge/evidence orchestration;
- qualification registry;
- truth adapters;
- audit/export APIs;
- role-based access;
- VPC/on-prem option;
- usage/evaluation capacity;
- support/SLA.

## Pricing

Annual platform commitment + usage/evaluation + premium deployment/support. Potential multi-year contracts.

## Objections

**"Why not build this internally?"**  
Carbon must show specialized evidence logic, faster adoption, reusable adapters, external network supply, and cross-program standardization.

**"Will this replace our CAE stack?"**  
No. Carbon sits as discovery/evidence infrastructure across upstream solvers/tools.

## Expansion

More seats/programs, higher usage, private discovery, OEM/API, lifecycle, qualification, intelligence.

## Recurrence

**VERY HIGH.** Main ARR engine.

## Network role

Network-connected where eligible programs use external discovery/evaluation. Enterprise UX remains conventional.

## Verdict

**CORE LONG-TERM SOFTWARE BUSINESS.** Requires successful repeat services before product-market fit can be claimed.

# 10. Opportunity H — Evidence API / OEM Rail

## ICP

CAE vendors, engineering AI platforms, digital-twin platforms, scientific software vendors, model-development tooling companies.

## Buyer

Product GM, platform VP, CTO, partnerships/business development, developer-platform owner.

## Trigger

- vendor wants independent evidence without building full V&V infrastructure;
- vendor needs qualification/provenance layer for generated models;
- vendor wants Carbon discovery embedded behind its own UX.

## Discovery questions

- Where in the product workflow should Carbon run?
- Is the vendor generating models, evaluating them, or both?
- What must remain white-labeled?
- What throughput/latency is required?
- Who owns end-customer evidence?
- What licensing/indemnity/support terms apply?
- Is revenue share or usage pricing preferable?

## Deliverables

- API/SDK;
- evidence/qualification endpoints;
- webhooks/status;
- audit bundles;
- OEM identity/tenant model;
- enterprise support.

## Pricing

Annual platform minimum + usage; optionally OEM royalty/revenue share.

## Objections

**"You could compete with us."**  
Position Carbon as neutral evidence/discovery rail, with contractual boundaries and no need to own customer-facing CAE workflow.

**"Why add another dependency?"**  
Must prove evidence credibility, customer trust, and reduced time-to-market outweigh integration cost.

## Expansion

Higher usage, more product lines, private discovery, qualification/lifecycle integration.

## Recurrence

**VERY HIGH** if embedded.

## Network role

Potentially powerful: vendors become demand aggregators for network evaluation/discovery without exposing crypto UX to their users.

## Verdict

**POTENTIALLY HIGHEST-SCALE DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL**, but not first sale.

# 11. Opportunity I — Sponsored Frontier Market

## ICP

Research sponsors, industrial consortia, government programs, CAE vendors, large engineering organizations with recurring open problems.

## Buyer

Program manager, R&D leader, innovation fund, government research office, ecosystem/partnership lead.

## Trigger

- explicit research frontier;
- desire for pay-for-progress funding;
- need for transparent challenge/bounty program;
- wish to access broad human/agent research supply.

## Discovery questions

- What exactly constitutes a frontier advance?
- What baseline is accepted?
- What reward pool is justified?
- What rights does sponsor need?
- Public or private participation?
- What is the program cadence?
- How should no-winner periods work?

## Deliverables

- qualified Challenge;
- marketplace campaign;
- participant onboarding;
- evaluation/frontier promotion;
- event-bound settlement;
- sponsor reporting;
- optional candidate/qualification downstream.

## Pricing

Program/platform fee + reward administration + evaluation usage; reward pool separately funded.

## Objections

**"Bounties attract noise."**  
Carbon's value is protected independent evaluation, not open submission alone.

**"How do we know winner wasn't lucky?"**  
Common frontier-promotion evidence and repeat policies.

## Expansion

Recurring portfolio sponsorship, enterprise platform, qualified-model programs.

## Recurrence

High for programmatic sponsors.

## Network role

**NETWORK-NATIVE FLAGSHIP.** Strong Alpha/network utility if real sponsor demand is routed through it.

## Verdict

**STRATEGIC MARKETPLACE END STATE**, dependent on treasury/frontier settlement qualification and sufficient participant liquidity.

# 12. Opportunity J — Model / Method Licensing

## ICP

Customers preferring ready-to-use qualified artifacts or proven construction methods rather than commissioning new R&D.

## Buyer

Product engineering, digital twin teams, software vendors, integrators.

## Trigger

- reusable method/model exists with rights that permit commercial licensing;
- customer's job aligns with qualified scope.

## Discovery questions

- Does customer's use match existing evidence?
- Exclusive or non-exclusive?
- Runtime/deployment needs?
- Source/weights/recipe rights?
- Requalification requirements?

## Deliverables

- licensed artifact/method;
- evidence package;
- deployment identity;
- usage rights;
- support/lifecycle.

## Pricing

Annual/perpetual/usage license + support; exclusivity premium if rights allow.

## Objection

**"Why not use an open model?"**  
Carbon must sell bounded evidence, support, integration, and known failure scope—not merely weights.

## Expansion

Support, lifecycle, broader licenses, OEM.

## Recurrence

Potentially high, but depends entirely on rights and product fit.

## Network role

Network may discover methods/models; OpCo licenses only where contractual rights permit.

## Verdict

**ATTRACTIVE OPTIONAL RAIL, NOT ASSUMED CORE REVENUE.** Rights uncertainty makes premature investor dependence inappropriate.

# 13. Opportunity K — Physics Intelligence / Experiment Allocation

## ICP

Mature Carbon customers with repeated ExperimentRecords and meaningful recurring decisions about what to try/test/qualify next.

## Buyer

R&D leader, simulation portfolio owner, digital engineering platform owner, chief scientist.

## Trigger

- enough repeated programs/evidence exist;
- decisions about next experiment are costly;
- Carbon has demonstrated prospective lift.

## Discovery questions

- What future decision would Carbon improve?
- What baseline decision process exists today?
- How is lift measured prospectively?
- What error cost is acceptable?
- Is the advice search guidance, experiment allocation, qualification routing, or deployment risk?

## Deliverables

- ranked recommendations;
- transfer/failure-risk predictions;
- experiment allocation plan;
- evidence/uncertainty and epistemic status.

## Pricing

Subscription, decision-support retainer, or usage. Should not be sold as generic "AI insights".

## Objections

**"Isn't this just analytics on your own data?"**  
It is commercially meaningful only if Carbon proves prospective decision improvement versus a credible baseline.

## Expansion

Enterprise-wide research planning, automated search, method library.

## Recurrence

Potentially very high.

## Network role

Network supplies diverse experiments; intelligence guides future program choices but remains outside the scientific judge.

## Verdict

**LATER HIGH-MARGIN MOAT PRODUCT. DO NOT SELL THE CLAIM BEFORE IT IS EARNED.**

# 15. Sales-stage architecture

Standard stages:

```text
S0 TARGET
S1 DISCOVERY
S2 TECHNICAL QUALIFICATION
S3 COMMERCIAL QUALIFICATION
S4 PROPOSED
S5 SECURITY / LEGAL / PROCUREMENT
S6 PILOT / CONTRACTED
S7 DELIVERED
S8 EXPANSION IDENTIFIED
S9 RECURRING / PLATFORM
```

Required CRM evidence by S3:

- buyer/champion;
- budget source;
- decision/job;
- truth-access mode;
- product family;
- requested deliverable;
- privacy/IP constraints;
- technical authority;
- timeline;
- quantified pain/value hypothesis;
- next-step owner.

Do not count an interesting technical conversation as pipeline without a buyer/problem/budget path.

# 16. Common first-call questions

1. What physical model or simulation workflow is expensive or difficult today?
2. Do you already have a fast model/surrogate?
3. What prevents you from relying on it more broadly?
4. What decision would become cheaper/faster if the model were credible?
5. What is the trusted reference today?
6. Which failure regimes matter most?
7. Who decides whether the model is acceptable?
8. What evidence would that person require?
9. Is the current problem evaluation, discovery, qualification, deployment, or lifecycle?
10. What data/solver/IP cannot leave your environment?
11. How often do models or operating conditions change?
12. What happens financially/operationally if the fast model is wrong?
13. What would make a 6–12 week pilot clearly successful?

## Track B — Energy / turbomachinery / industrial physics

High repeated-simulation value, strong surrogate/digital-twin use, potentially easier commercial procurement than defense.

Maintain a parallel strategic partner track for CAE/software vendors as future OEM/API distribution.
