## Evidence every first pilot needs

Use the #956 framework and #959 sample to agree the intended decision, customer-owned requirements and independent evidence. Establish the target population P separately from sampling Q and weighting w; distinguish cell/reference validation from surrogate/reference agreement. Specify physical referents and uncertainty only where the intended claim requires them. Record source/runtime/artifact identities, independence/conflicts, rights and approved disclosure. Retain false-feasible decisions, attacks, unresolved reference results and missing measurements in the audit's conclusion.

For a customer EvidenceAudit, a failed subject model can be an accepted deliverable. An unqualified or inadequate exam/reference cannot certify a subject. The sample currently supplies development evidence and gaps; none of these prospects changes its eligible tier. Automotive functional safety, tool qualification, production validation and the sample's NASA indexing are separate; no reviewed source says NASA-STD-7009B is mandatory for these buyers.

## Conversation recommendation — owner action only

Bring the public adverse-evidence page and a short scope proposal. Ask which model and version chooses a charge protocol today, which failure would change the decision, how the team currently validates it, and which independent evidence would be accepted. Request their incumbent reference/model/baseline, limits, uncertainty and artifact-use rights before offering a comparison. Establish who accepts physics, who owns BMS calibration and who pays; those may be different roles.

Discuss the independent assessment first. A qualified fast model, deployment or recurring re-evidence offer needs the framework's later-tier prerequisites. No message has been sent. Any outreach, commercial terms, customer commitments or adoption are the owner's decisions.
