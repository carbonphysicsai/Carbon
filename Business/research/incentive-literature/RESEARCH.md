## Protocol and subnet policy are separate

A subnet decides admissibility and how evaluation becomes a weight matrix. Yuma combines eligible validators' rows with active stake. In its classical description, the consensus cap for a miner is the highest weight supported by at least κ of active stake; excess weight is clipped. Normalized stake-weighted clipped rank gives miner incentive. Validator trust measures retained row weight, not evaluator accuracy. Bonds remember validator support; dividends reward that support when the miner earns incentive. These are economic agreement signals. They do not inspect Carbon's physics, hidden exam or buyer decision. [Official consensus description](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/internals/consensus).

The implementation must be versioned. The inspected [Subtensor epoch source](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/5c6e83e2f4936f257234f246957f75684c35227c/pallets/subtensor/src/epoch/run_epoch.rs) has classical and `Yuma3On` branches, active/permit filtering, row normalization, bond updates and integer emission apportionment. In the classical branch, instant bonds are stake-weighted bond weights normalized by miner column; an exponential moving average retains history. Dividends use bonds and incentive. The Yuma3 branch computes and normalizes bonds differently and includes active stake in dividends. Do not replay the classical equation against a Yuma3 receipt. Also distinguish the subnet's own moving average of its proposed weights from the chain's moving average of bonds.

Per-block pool allocation and per-epoch participant settlement are different layers. Official emissions documentation describes owner/miner/validator allocations of SOURCED 18/41/41 percent, liquid alpha conditional on Yuma3, registration/permit filtering, and epoch-end recipients. Those figures are documentation values (L=B=H each), **not a read of Carbon's deployed chain**. Zero valid weights and zero incentive have fallback behavior; default and null-consensus paths need explicit replay. The current page describes price-EMA/burn-adjusted allocation; older dTAO and TaoFlow explanations have used other allocation rules. No network-wide allocation formula or current token price is adopted here. [Official emissions page](https://www.bittensor.com/docs/concepts/emissions), [pinned epoch source](https://github.com/opentensor/subtensor/blob/5c6e83e2f4936f257234f246957f75684c35227c/pallets/subtensor/src/epoch/run_epoch.rs).

The metagraph's emission amount must be interpreted using its exact runtime/API units and epoch, not as a per-block rate or fiat payment. UID is a recyclable slot; bind recipient hotkey at settlement. Burn-target semantics, owner-hotkey withholding, eligibility and participant budget can change realized payment even when submitted weights look right. Network value and OpCo revenue are outside Carbon's scientific score. [Business Canon](../../Business_Canon.md), [Constitution C0 override](../../../CONSTITUTION.md).

## Failure cases and anti-copy tradeoffs

Opentensor's [May 2024 weight-copying working paper](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/papers/BT_Weight_Copier-29May2024.pdf) reports validators could copy evaluation work and receive equal or greater credit. Its commit-reveal proposal makes current weights unavailable during a delay. This is evidence of a free-rider failure and a proposed remedy, not proof every deployed subnet is protected. Paper URL redirects on a fresh open at this cut-off; the indexed paper title/date/abstract were available. Record that retrieval limitation when reusing it.

The [consensus-based weights paper](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/papers/BT-Consensus-based-Weights.pdf) studies adaptive bond smoothing and delayed copying with modeled and historical signals. Its benefit depends on changing miner quality and copy delay; static agreement eventually erodes first-mover advantage. Treat it as conditional research, not a universal percentage improvement or current chain parameter. The [CCS poster](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/papers/ACM_CCS2024_Poster.pdf) is an indexed historical corroboration; its URL also redirects, so no new detailed quantitative claim is taken from it.

The subnet authors' [SN37 v3.0.5 release](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/finetuning/releases/tag/v3.0.5) documents a dataloader bug and increased sample size to reduce validator variance. [v3.1.0](https://github.com/macrocosm-os/finetuning/releases/tag/v3.1.0) changes cadence/smoothing and resets state. These are concrete evaluation/coordination failures and repairs. A release is not an independent post-repair effectiveness study.

Recommendations for the optimizer's simulation, not Carbon policy:

- Distinguish copying **validator weights** from copying **miner model artifacts**, and from independently discovering a similar good method. Hash equality detects exact bytes; response similarity, parameter similarity and provenance can be noisy. An accusation needs its own policy, evidence and appeal path; scientific quality cannot be inferred from originality.
- Include stake concentration and validator abstention. Majority-stake agreement assumptions fail under correlated evaluators or captured stake. Yuma does not turn a single validator into independent validation.
- Include multiple hotkeys sharing a construction, registration-time resets, tie flooding and UID replacement. Fee/stake costs constrain attacks; they do not establish unique human builders. Never let a new hotkey reset model age without the registered lineage rule.
- Compare zero-decay incumbent protection with decay-to-burn and replacement-only reward. Decay increases pressure to improve but can remove the incentive to keep serving the best available model. Resetting the clock on tiny self-improvements can reward gaming; refusing any self-improvement reset can penalize productive incumbents. These are hypotheses to measure.
- Track scientific gate failure before ranking. Do not reward an inadmissible but cheaper model, or use customer payment, sponsor size, copied-weight agreement or fee as scientific merit.
