## Buyer-side evidence: useful but limited

[Harcourt Industrial](https://www.simscale.com/customers/harcourt-industrial/) says outsourced aerospace-fixture FEA often cost **tens of thousands of dollars per project**, and describes **60 days** to full productivity after moving in-house. The amount is qualitative: numerical endpoints and midpoint remain unknown. This is a vendor-hosted customer account, not an invoice or a surrogate offer.

[European Gravitational Observatory](https://www.simscale.com/customers/european-gravitational-observatory/) reports avoided consultancy cost **up to EUR 50,000/project** and engineering time **up to 3 months**. These are upper-bound savings; low/base and actual purchased fee are unknown. [DMG](https://www.simscale.com/customers/dmg/) reports **35% labor savings** per project versus outsourced data-centre CFD, with no absolute price. None establishes Carbon's ROI, model price or willingness to pay.

## Delivery target interpretation

Do not collapse quote latency, installation, training, repeat inference, completed analysis and accepted reusable product. Public evidence supports days for some ready-input studies and weeks for integration, while initial reusable-tool delivery remains poorly specified. [Proposed targets](JOB_OFFERS.md) therefore start **after an accepted data-readiness receipt**. Record calendar waiting separately from expert hours, CPU/GPU hours and training duration.

Public price discovery here is bounded by accessible official product pages, government listings, original supplier interviews and supplier/customer case studies. An inaccessible price is an explicit retrieval gap. The absence of a number in this set is not proof that no buyer can obtain a published or negotiated price.
